Using a self-custody wallet means taking on risks that a bank account does not have. Please read this page before you put money into {{appName}}. It does not cover every possible risk.

> Cryptoassets are high-risk. You can lose all the money you put in, and you are unlikely to be protected if something goes wrong. {{company}} is not authorised or regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Nothing here is financial advice.

## 1. You are the only one with the keys

- Your recovery phrase is the only backup of your wallet. If you lose it and lose your device, your funds are gone for good.
- Anyone who sees your recovery phrase can take everything. Do not photograph it, store it in notes or cloud storage, or type it into any website.
- We cannot reset, recover or look up your phrase. Nobody can.

## 2. Transfers cannot be undone

- A transfer on the blockchain is final. There is no chargeback, no cancel button and nobody to appeal to.
- Funds sent to a wrong address are lost unless the owner of that address chooses to return them.

## 3. Wrong network, wrong token

- {{brand}} supports only USDT (TRC-20) and TRX on TRON.
- USDT also exists on other networks, such as Ethereum (ERC-20) and BNB Chain (BEP-20). USDT sent to your {{brand}} address on another network will not arrive and is normally lost.
- When sending to an exchange, make sure the exchange expects USDT on TRON.

## 4. Scams

- **Address poisoning.** Scammers send tiny transfers from addresses that look like ones you have used, hoping you will copy the wrong one from your history. Always check the whole address, not just the first and last characters.
- **Fake support.** Nobody from {{brand}} will ever message you first, or ask for your recovery phrase, PIN or a "verification" transfer.
- **Fake apps and websites.** Install the app only from the App Store link on kastrel.xyz.
- If you are tricked into sending a transfer, it cannot be reversed.

## 5. USDT is controlled by its issuer

- USDT is issued by Tether, an independent company. We have no connection with it.
- The issuer can freeze or destroy USDT at any address. It has done so many times, often at the request of law enforcement. If your address is frozen, we cannot help.
- USDT that has passed through addresses linked to crime may be refused by exchanges, even if you received it innocently.
- USDT is designed to track the US dollar, but it can trade below one dollar, and could lose value entirely if its issuer fails.
- Holding USDT may not give you any right to redeem it for dollars from the issuer.

## 6. Network fees and TRX

- Every USDT transfer on TRON costs a network fee, paid in TRX. If you hold USDT but no TRX, you cannot send it.
- The fee is set by the network and can rise. The figure the app shows is an estimate.
- TRX is a volatile cryptoasset and its price can fall sharply.

## 7. The TRON network

- The app depends on the TRON network. Congestion, outages, software faults, attacks or rule changes on the network can delay or prevent transfers, or cause loss.
- We do not operate or control the network.

## 8. Third-party services

- The app reads balances and sends transfers through a third-party network provider, and gets prices from third-party data services. If they are down, slow or wrong, the app may show out-of-date or incorrect information, or be unable to send.
- Prices and charts are indicative only.

## 9. Software

- The app may contain faults despite our testing. Faults in wallet software can cause loss.
- A compromised device (for example one with malware or a modified operating system) can expose your keys.
- Keep iOS and the app up to date.

## 10. Law and tax

- Rules on cryptoassets differ between countries and change often. Using a wallet or holding USDT may be restricted or banned where you live.
- You may owe tax on your transactions. That is your responsibility.
- A change in the law could force us to change or withdraw the app. Your funds would still be on the network and could be restored in other TRON wallet software with your recovery phrase.

## 11. No protection schemes

Cryptoassets held in a self-custody wallet are not deposits. They are not covered by the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme or any similar scheme, and the Financial Ombudsman Service cannot consider complaints about them.
